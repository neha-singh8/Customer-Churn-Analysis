# Customer-Churn-Analysis

## Project Overview
- This project analyzes a dataset of 64,374 customers to understand key drivers of churn and revenue loss. It explores usage behavior, payment delays, support interactions, and subscription patterns to identify high-risk customers and improve retention strategies.

## Dataset Source
- Source: Kaggle

## Tools used
- SQL (data cleaning, data transformation, exploratory data analysis)
- Tableau (interactive dashboards and data visualization)

## Business Questions
- Which customer segments show the highest churn risk?
- At what point do payment delays significantly increase churn likelihood?
- How does usage intensity affect long-term retention?
- Do frequent support interactions indicate churn risk?
- Why do long-tenure customers still churn?
- Which behavioral patterns best predict churn risk?
- How does declining engagement relate to churn probability?
- Which high-value segments contribute most to churn-driven revenue loss?
- How effective are current pricing and subscription models in retention?
- Which operational issues are linked to higher churn?

## Key Insights
- 64,374 customers analyzed, with 30,493 customers churned. Overall churn rate is 47.4% 
- Churned users represent ~$15.8M in lost revenue - a major revenue leak.
- Female customers churn more (55.0%) than males (38.6%) indicating possible engagement gap.
- Age has little impact on churn, behavior matters more than demographics.
- Customers with 25+ months tenure churn more (~55–56%), possible long-term dissatisfaction.
- Low usage (<6 times) strongly drives churn (61.8%).
- Payment delays are the strongest churn driver (64–76% for 15+ days), financial friction is critical.
- High support usage (6+ calls) links to ~60% churn suggesting unresolved issues.
- Subscription type has little effect (~46–48%), suggests pricing alone doesn’t prevent churn.
- Monthly contracts churn more (51.6%) - weaker long-term commitment.
- Last interaction timing has weak impact, not a strong retention signal.
- Combined risks of low usage, delays and support calls push churn above 70%.
- Active, on-time customers show lowest churn, shows strong engagement is key.


## Recommendations (What companies can do)
- Reduce payment delays via reminders/auto-pay, this can cut churn by 10–25% (McKinsey).  
- Improve onboarding & engagement for low users as it boosts retention by 20–50% (Bain).  
- Strengthen support quality as ~70% of churn is service-related (Zendesk).    
- Focus on long-tenure users through retention campaigns as they have up to 5x higher lifetime value (Harvard Business Review).  
- Promote annual plans over monthly as it can reduce churn by 30–60% (Recurly).  
- Use early warning systems as predictive models improve retention by 20–40% (Gartner).  
- Run proactive win-back programs as they recover 10–25% of at-risk users (McKinsey).  
- Don’t rely only on subscription type — engagement matters more (Deloitte).  
- Shift focus from acquisition to retention,as +5% retention can boost profit by 25–95% (Bain).  
## Conclusion
Churn is mainly driven by usage, payment delays and support issues rather than demographics or subscription type. The business has a large at-risk customer base, and improving engagement, support, and payment experience can significantly reduce churn and boost profitability.
